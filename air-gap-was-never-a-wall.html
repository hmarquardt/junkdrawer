The Air Gap Was Never a Wall.

A computer with no network cable is harder to reach. It is not removed from physics. The strange part of the “CPU heat” story is not that it works. The strange part is how long we have known that supposedly isolated machines can still leak and receive information.
Published September 18, 2026~9 minute readEvidence separated from inferenceSingle-file interactive artifact
The assertion

Laughing at two air-gapped computers communicating through heat gets the history backwards.

OpenAI researcher Noam Brown used exactly that example on the Dwarkesh Podcast: nearby air-gapped machines can, in academic demonstrations, communicate by varying CPU heat and observing temperature sensors. He explicitly called the work “mostly academic.” The mechanism is not hypothetical. BitWhisper demonstrated it in 2015.

An air gap is an architectural boundary — not a law of physics.
The historical arc
83

years separate Bell Labs’ 1943 discovery of compromising emanations from today’s AI-and-SDR-assisted signal reconstruction.

The public record already spans electromagnetic leakage, acoustic leakage, RF implants, retro-reflectors, USB air-gap bridges, optical channels, thermal channels and machine-learning reconstruction.

01

The joke only works if the premise is impossible.

It isn’t. The cleanest way to think about the debate is as an evidence ladder.

Can information cross an air gap without Ethernet/Wi-Fi?Yes. Many mechanisms have been demonstrated.Established
Can two nearby computers communicate through heat?Yes. BitWhisper demonstrated bidirectional thermal signaling between compromised machines.Demonstrated
Have intelligence services built tools to bridge isolated networks?Yes. Leaked CIA and NSA-era documents describe USB/RF air-gap bridging and covert collection from disconnected systems.Documented
Do we know NSA or CIA operationally used CPU-temperature signaling?No public evidence establishes that specific technique.Unknown
Would the classified state of the art be frozen at 2008–2016?Almost certainly not — but the details are an inference, not a leaked fact.Strong inference

Brown’s point was not that an AI is going to transmit a model checkpoint by warming a CPU. It was that isolation does not eliminate every possible communication channel.

That narrower claim is supported by decades of security research.
Public fact

BitWhisper, 2015

Researchers demonstrated 1–8 bits/hour over 0–40 cm using only heat generation and onboard temperature sensors. Slow, yes. Zero, no.

Public fact

Brown, 2026

The OpenAI researcher described the temperature example as academic evidence that air-gapping alone may not be an absolute containment guarantee.

Important limit

No thermal NSA manual

There is no public leak proving a U.S. intelligence agency deployed BitWhisper-style thermal signaling in the field.

02

This problem predates modern computers.

The core discovery is ancient by computing standards: information-processing equipment interacts with the physical world, and those interactions can betray information.

1943

Bell Labs finds compromising emanations.

During wartime work on secure equipment, engineers discover that electrical signals can reveal information being processed. This becomes the historical root of what would later be called TEMPEST.

1950s–1960s

TEMPEST becomes an organized government discipline.

NSA’s declassified histories describe dedicated investigation of compromising emanations, including electrical, acoustic and other physical leakage from equipment processing classified information.

1980s

Van Eck makes display leakage public.

Public researchers show that electromagnetic radiation from display systems can be reconstructed at a distance — a civilian demonstration of a problem governments had treated seriously for decades.

2008–2009

Snowden-era ANT documents show hardware built for air-gap bridging.

COTTONMOUTH is described as a USB hardware implant with a wireless bridge into target networks and explicit air-gap-bridging capability. RAGEMASTER uses RF illumination and backscatter to assist video collection.

2014–2015

Academia turns ordinary components into covert transmitters.

AirHopper uses FM-range electromagnetic emissions; BitWhisper uses heat. The catalog of possible physical channels begins to look less like a curiosity and more like a field.

2016

CIA’s Brutal Kangaroo explicitly targets closed networks.

A leaked CIA user guide describes a suite for “air gap jumping using thumbdrives” and creating a covert command-and-control network inside the isolated environment.

2024

Deep learning improves TEMPEST reconstruction.

Deep-TEMPEST applies neural methods to weak HDMI emanations, substantially improving reconstruction compared with prior digital-display approaches.

2025

TEMPEST-LoRa reaches commodity infrastructure.

Researchers demonstrate covert transmissions from video-cable emanations that commercial LoRa receivers can decode, with reported tests reaching 87.5 meters or 21.6 kbps under experimental conditions.

2025–2026

The U.S. government still treats emanations as a live problem.

DCSA asks industry for “current and emerging technologies” to mitigate compromising electromagnetic emanations affecting classified and sensitive information.

03

The leap from “demonstrated” to “operational” already happened.

Not for every exotic channel. But leaked documents establish that intelligence agencies have intentionally engineered around air gaps and physical emanations.

CIA · Documented

Brutal Kangaroo

A 2016 CIA Engineering Development Group guide describes a tool suite for targeting closed networks by moving through thumb drives. One component, Shadow, is described as persistence distributed across the closed network that acts as a covert command-and-control network.

This is conventional removable-media bridging rather than an exotic thermal/RF side channel — which matters because intelligence operators generally prefer the simplest technique that reliably works.

NSA-era leak · Documented

COTTONMOUTH

The leaked ANT catalog describes a USB implant providing a wireless bridge into a target network, explicit air-gap bridging, persistence, reprogramming and an RF link for commands and data.

The ANT catalog surfaced during the Snowden era; its precise source provenance has been debated. The catalog itself is widely treated as authentic NSA material.

NSA-era leak · Documented

RAGEMASTER

A tiny RF retro-reflector hidden in a VGA cable is described as assisting remote collection of the target’s analog video signal. The leaked product sheet labels it operational.

NSA history · Declassified

TEMPEST as a standing discipline

NSA’s own histories describe government work on compromising emanations going back to the 1940s and expanding beyond cryptographic equipment to computers and other information-processing systems.

Game theory is not proof — but it changes the prior.

If a useful class of techniques has existed for decades, has been institutionalized, and leaked documents show purpose-built operational hardware, it is reasonable to expect continued classified R&D. What it does not justify is claiming a particular unobserved technique was deployed.

04

There is no single “air-gap channel.”

The real lesson is that computers share a physical environment. Any controllable physical effect that another sensor can observe is, in principle, a candidate communication path.

📡

Electromagnetic / RF

Video cables, memory buses, processors and other digital electronics radiate. Some research deliberately shapes those emissions; other work reconstructs information that leaks unintentionally.

🌡️

Thermal

Workload controls heat. Nearby sensors can observe temperature changes. BitWhisper turned that elementary fact into a bidirectional, extremely low-bandwidth channel.

💡

Optical

Status LEDs and displays can vary faster or more subtly than a human observer notices, creating a path to cameras or photodiodes.

🔊

Acoustic / ultrasonic

Fans, speakers and other components can generate sound; microphones and modern wearable/phone sensors can become receivers.

🧲

Magnetic / electrical / power

Current draw and switching activity produce magnetic and electrical effects. Research has examined power lines and magnetic fields as unconventional signaling paths.

🔌

Hardware bridges

Sometimes the cleverest “side channel” is not a side channel at all: a modified cable, USB device, peripheral or other implant that quietly creates the missing communications link.

The security question is not “Can I transfer gigabytes this way?” It is “Can any information cross the boundary at all?”

For command-and-control, synchronization, a trigger or a tiny secret, very low bandwidth can still matter.
05

So what probably exists in 2026?

This is where the receipts end and the extrapolation begins. The projection below is intentionally bounded by capabilities already visible in public research and commercial hardware.

Pre-positioned access
Environment discovery
Channel selection
Short, quiet signaling
Normal network exfiltration outside the boundary
Strong inference

Adaptive, multimodal implants

Rather than committing to “the thermal trick,” a sophisticated tool could survey available hardware and conditions, then choose among RF, optical, acoustic, thermal or peripheral-assisted paths. The individual primitives are public; combining them is an engineering step, not new physics.

Public trend → inference

ML-assisted weak-signal recovery

Deep-TEMPEST shows the direction clearly: machine learning can improve reconstruction from noisy electromagnetic leakage. Modern SDRs, GPUs and learned signal models make faint, messy emissions more useful than they were in the classic TEMPEST era.

Strong inference

Asymmetric links

Inbound and outbound communication need not use the same mechanism. A tiny, robust command path could wake or task pre-positioned software; a different channel could carry the response. The system may remain silent almost all the time.

Strong inference

Use the receivers already in the room

Modern environments are dense with radios and sensors: phones, watches, Bluetooth devices, cameras, microphones, building systems and IoT equipment. TEMPEST-LoRa is a public example of the broader idea that commodity infrastructure may sometimes serve as the receiving side.

Strong inference

Miniaturization changes operations

Computation, storage, radios, sensors and cryptography now fit in tiny low-power packages. Even without a classified breakthrough, 2026 hardware can be smaller, smarter and more autonomous than the implants revealed from the late-2000s era.

Operational reality

The exotic path only has to cross the hard part

The physical covert channel may only need to cover a desk, room or secure perimeter. Once information reaches a compromised networked device outside that boundary, ordinary communications can take over.

Reasonable conclusion

The classified state of the art almost certainly includes air-gap and emanations research more advanced than the tools publicly revealed from 2008–2016, because the underlying sensing, radios, computation and signal-processing ecosystem has advanced dramatically.

Conclusion the evidence does not support

“NSA definitely has a deployed AI-controlled thermal mesh that does X.” We do not have the public evidence to name a specific undisclosed capability, deployment or target.

The likely frontier is not a magic trick. It is orchestration.

The interesting 2026 capability is probably the combination: pre-positioned access + sensing + adaptive channel choice + modern signal reconstruction + extreme patience. That is a much more plausible evolution of the 80-year TEMPEST lineage than a single exotic transmission method becoming the universal solution.

Bottom line

Mocking the CPU-heat example as physically absurd is indefensible. Treating it as evidence that a specific intelligence service currently uses that exact technique would also be indefensible. The interesting — and supportable — conclusion sits between those two extremes: air-gap exploitation is real, old, operationally relevant, and still evolving.
