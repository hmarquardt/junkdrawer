About the Safari

A living field journal built from the road, the trail, the lake edge, and the quiet moments when something wild appears.

What is this?

Hank & Heather's Wilderness Safari is our living field journal — a map of the places we’ve wandered, driven, hiked, paused, squinted into the brush, and said, “Wait… what was that?”

Some of these sightings are close to home. Some come from trips farther out. Together they tell the story of two curious people following birdsong, turtle crossings, muddy tracks, strange calls, roadside surprises, lake edges, river bottoms, and the small wild moments that usually vanish unless you stop long enough to notice them.

This page ignores the formal trip structure and focuses on the animals themselves: what we saw, where we saw it, what we said into the recorder, what the weather was doing, and any photos we captured along the way. It is part map, part scrapbook, part nature log, and part excuse to keep going outside.

What counts as Safari?

Birds count. Turtles count. Snakes absolutely count. Weird bugs count. Mystery noises count provisionally. Anything that makes us stop the car, grab the camera, or argue lovingly about identification probably counts.

Privacy & Data

This page is read-only and public-facing. It displays observation records from the wildlife field recorder, but does not allow edits, uploads, deletes, or administrative changes.
