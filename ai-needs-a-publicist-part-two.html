AI needs a publicist
Part two · September 2026

THE AI PANIC
NEEDS A FACT-CHECK.

Yes, AI creates real risks. No, the evidence does not say “the machines are about to kill us.” And no, the companies asking to write the rules are not disinterested referees.

The honest position: accelerate the benefits, engineer against demonstrated harms, and refuse to let either panic or corporate lobbying decide who gets to build the future.
RISK
≠
DOOM
A dangerous tool is not an autonomous apocalypse.
REAL

Misuse is happening.

Scams, hacking, surveillance and weapons engineering are legitimate problems worth addressing.

HUMAN

People remain in charge.

They choose the targets, provide access and hardware, deploy the output and decide what happens next.

MISSING

The extinction bridge.

Current misuse does not prove recursive self-improvement, loss of control or human extinction this decade.

AI is changing the economics of malicious activity more than the fundamental nature of it.

The one-sentence reality check
Read the report, not the panic

WHAT ANTHROPIC
ACTUALLY FOUND.

The report is serious. It is also much narrower than the political story built around it.

The evidence supports

  • AI can make hackers faster and less skilled operators more capable.
  • Agents can perform reconnaissance, write code, execute tools and process stolen data.
  • Governments can use AI to scale surveillance and propaganda.
  • Weapons engineers can compress design, simulation and documentation cycles.
  • Centralized providers need better defenses against stolen keys, proxies and fraud.

The evidence does not establish

  • An AI independently choosing to attack anyone.
  • A novel cyber technique unavailable before AI.
  • A successful operational autonomous weapons system created by Claude.
  • An imminent AI-enabled biological attack.
  • AI propaganda meaningfully changing elections or mass opinion.
  • A path from today's agents to human extinction by 2030.

The report's own caveats

These are not skeptical interpretations imposed from outside. They are admissions in Anthropic's document.

Selection

The cases are described as the “most notable and novel” examples—not a representative sample. There is no denominator showing how common they are.

Cyber

Anthropic says none of the reported campaigns depended on an entirely novel attack technique. The ingredients remained stolen credentials, phishing, unpatched systems and exposed services.

Agency

Humans retained the decisions that mattered most: target selection, monetization and review of results. Autonomy increased throughput; it did not determine severity.

Influence

Most influence operations drew little or no authentic engagement. The broadest reach came from pre-existing state-media distribution.

Biology

Anthropic explicitly says the cases are not evidence of imminent biological threats currently uplifted by Claude.

The regulatory-capture problem

Why nobody gets sued

Not suing a critic is not proof of orchestration. It is the rational legal and public-relations choice.

01

Opinion is not a trade secret.

“This is unsafe” and “AI could kill us” are predictions and policy arguments—not readily actionable factual disclosures.

02

Discovery cuts both ways.

A lawsuit could expose internal forecasts, evaluations and executive communications the company would rather keep private.

03

The warning helps the brand.

Anthropic sells itself as the frontier lab that takes catastrophic risk seriously. Silencing the messenger would contradict the pitch.

The geopolitical layer

AMERICA BUILDS THE MODEL.
CHINA BUILDS THE ECOSYSTEM.

The strategic contest is not simply who has the smartest chatbot. It is who becomes the foundation beneath everyone else's AI.

01Access US frontier models
02Harvest reasoning and outputs
03Distill cheaper models
04Release usable weights
05Win global adoption

Anthropic attributes this pattern to several Chinese labs. Those are first-party intelligence allegations, not findings independently tested in court.

Inside China

Control the service

  • Model and algorithm filing
  • Security assessments
  • Content and political controls
  • State access and industrial direction
  • Domestic technological sovereignty
Outside China

Diffuse the weights

  • Cheap local deployment
  • Permissive modification
  • Global South partnerships
  • Reduced dependence on US APIs
  • Chinese tooling and standards underneath

China does not need every company to buy its chatbot. It needs the world to build on its foundation.

The open-weight strategy

The own goal

If Washington turns frontier AI into a licensed utility operated by three companies, America may keep the best closed model while losing the developers, derivatives and global defaults.

MOAT

Compliance favors giants.

Large labs can afford government relations, evaluations, legal teams and approval delays. Startups often cannot.

GAP

A US pause is not a global pause.

Code, techniques and trained researchers cross borders more easily than legislation does.

DEFAULT

Developers choose what exists.

If strong American open models disappear, downloadable Chinese models become the practical alternative.

The contested territory

OPEN MODELS ARE
POWER WITHOUT PERMISSION.

That is their greatest democratic value—and their irreducible risk.

The freedom case

Why openness matters

  • Private, local inference
  • Independent inspection and research
  • Competition with entrenched providers
  • Access for universities and small businesses
  • National and organizational sovereignty
  • No single company can revoke the future
The control problem

What cannot be recalled

  • Safety tuning can be removed
  • Sanctioned actors can run models offline
  • No provider sees malicious prompts
  • No account can be terminated
  • Military fine-tuning remains private
  • A released weight file persists indefinitely

First, use the right words

Most “open-source AI” is really open-weight AI. The parameters are downloadable, but the complete training data, training code and reproducible process may not be available.

Closed API

Centralized, monitorable and revocable—but opaque, dependent and controlled by one provider.

Open weights

Inspectible, customizable and sovereign—but distributable, modifiable and effectively permanent.

The question is not whether open AI has risks. It is whether concentrating AI in three boardrooms is safer.

The question panic skips

The strongest case for openness is not that everyone is trustworthy. It is that no institution is trustworthy enough to own general intelligence for everyone else.

Acceleration with guardrails

REGULATE THE HARM.
DON'T LICENSE THE FUTURE.

Pro-AI does not mean pretending risk is imaginary. It means demanding rules tied to evidence, conduct and measurable capability.

Punish harmful acts, regardless of tool

Fraud, unauthorized access, stalking, sanctions evasion and weapons crimes do not become acceptable because AI was involved. Enforce the conduct.

Require incident disclosure for frontier providers

Material breaches, escaped agents and verified catastrophic-capability findings should not depend on voluntary corporate storytelling.

Measure marginal risk

Compare an open model with closed APIs, existing software and public information. Do not attribute every old danger newly performed with AI to open weights.

Use capability thresholds, not spooky vocabulary

“Superintelligence” is politically powerful but legally mushy. Controls should attach to reproducible demonstrations of exceptional capability.

Keep the referee independent

A company seeking a regulatory moat should not define the tests, grade itself and decide which competitors are allowed to operate.

Protect an American open-weight lane

If every capable downloadable model comes from China, the United States will have traded a theoretical safety advantage for a strategic dependency.

Build defense at the same speed as offense

Use AI for vulnerability discovery, patching, biological monitoring, fraud detection and intelligence analysis. The answer to adversarial AI is not less defensive AI.

BUILD FAST.
TEST HARD.
SHOW YOUR WORK.

A confident society does not respond to every powerful invention with denial—or surrender. It learns, competes and governs what actually happens.

Primary documents and reporting
