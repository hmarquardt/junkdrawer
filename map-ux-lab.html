🔥 Map Torture Test 0/11

        
    
        
    
          
        
    
      
    

      
    
        
    Below-map content 1 — scroll-through range
    
        
    This region exists so you can roll the wheel or swipe through the map: content above, map in the middle, content below. A well-behaved embedded map lets page scrolling continue when the wheel gesture was not meant for it (or after an explicit opt-in gesture); an aggressive map eats every wheel tick and traps your reading flow. Compare both engines in each scroll mode and note where your hand hesitates.
    
        
    Pay attention to the moment the cursor crosses the map border while you are still reading above-the-map content. A calm map should feel like scrolling past a picture; a greedy map feels like a speed bump.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Below-map content 2 — touch and trackpad reality
    
        
    On a trackpad, a two-finger scroll produces a stream of high-frequency wheel events with small deltas; on a mouse, one notch produces a single large delta. Engines handle these very differently. Watch the wheel telemetry in the instrumentation strip while scrolling through the map — Leaflet exposes px-per-zoom and debounce; MapLibre hard-codes its wheel curve. That difference is one of the concrete levers this lab exposes.
    
        
    On a phone, one finger over a map usually pans the map instead of the page — that trade-off is exactly what cooperative gesture modes negotiate.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Below-map content 3 — what to write down
    
        
    For each engine note: (1) how many wheel notches a “useful” zoom step took, (2) whether you accidentally zoomed while trying to scroll, (3) whether inertia carried the map further than you wanted, (4) whether pinch zoomed without accidentally rotating (MapLibre only — Leaflet cannot rotate), and (5) whether the USER HAS TAKEN CONTROL indicator appeared the moment you intended it to.
    
        
    Then press ★ This Feels Right on whichever configuration felt invisible — good map UX is the configuration you stop noticing.
