Return-to-playing rules
  • Gentle arm and shoulder movement first; then light notes. Don't force cold stretches.
  • Use the minimum fretting pressure that makes a clear note. No death grip.
  • Let the thumb support, not squeeze. Keep wrists as neutral as practical.
  • Rest 3–5 minutes after roughly 20–25 minutes of playing, sooner if needed.
  • Sharp pain, numbness, tingling or persistent weakness means stop, not push through. Seek professional advice if symptoms persist.

Return-to-play guidance: BAPAM · Berklee PULSE. This is skill practice, not medical rehabilitation.

Progression refreshers

Pop/rock: G – D – Em – C. Minor mood: Am – Fmaj7 – C – G. Blues-ready: E7 – A7 – B7. Play each for four beats, then two.

Barre chords? Not yet necessary.

The F and Bm barre shapes are in the deck for later weeks. Start with Fmaj7 or partial F, avoid clamping harder, and treat thumb/wrist comfort as the priority.

How scales become music

Root: the home note. Third: helps make the major/minor sound. Fifth: stable support. Pentatonic: five notes, useful for rock and blues. Blues scale: adds a passing “blue note.” Use slides, bends and vibrato only when the hand is comfortable again.

Picking & technique

Equal attention to both hands. Start with tiny, economical movements and an even sound. Fingerstyle is here too: p = thumb, i = index, m = middle, a = ring.

Tension audit
Relaxation is technique.
  • Check your jaw, shoulder, thumb and wrist between repetitions.
  • Try fretting a note firmly, then release pressure until it buzzes; add only enough pressure to make it clear.
  • Alternate down/up strokes on one string before crossing strings.
  • For 1–2–3–4 patterns, use a small comfortable fret span; do not force a wide stretch.
  • When picking gets uneven, lower the BPM and start again—not harder, just cleaner.
Your goal isn't raw grip strength. It's precise movement with less effort, followed by gradually increasing tolerance.

Two clean synthetic notes. Listen for the distance, then reveal the answer. No microphone and no song recordings needed.

Theory memory joggers


String & note names
Low to high: E A D G B e. Musical alphabet: A B C D E F G, then repeat. Natural semitones: B–C and E–F; most other neighboring letters are whole steps.


Chord anatomy
Major triad = 1–3–5; minor = 1–♭3–5. Power chord = 1–5. A dominant seventh adds ♭7, giving that blues tension.


Build your ear
Hum a root, sing its fifth, then find the pitches on the guitar. In the key of C: C D E F G A B. The A minor pentatonic contains A C D E G.
